Profiles of Ed Craven, the Australian co-founder of the crypto casino Stake and the streaming platform Kick, often mention his father, Jamie Craven, and the collapse of Spedley Securities. One popular version, which also appears on Ed Craven's Wikipedia page, says the elder Craven was "jailed for six months in the 1980s" over that collapse.

The official record tells a more specific story. The collapse happened in 1989, but the criminal cases came years later. According to the corporate regulator ASIC, James (Jamie) Craven pleaded guilty in June 1995 and was sentenced to nine months' imprisonment.

This piece sets out what the primary and reputable sources show and leaves out everything else. It makes no claim about Ed Craven's conduct. Nothing in the record suggests he had any part in events that happened before he was born.

## What Spedley was and how it collapsed

Spedley Securities was a Sydney merchant bank headed by Brian Yuill. It was the "public face" of a group of companies that collapsed in 1989, after the October 1987 sharemarket crash.

The best primary source is ASIC's "Report of the Investigation into Spedley Securities, Greater Pacific, GPI Leisure and other companies." According to that report:

- The events investigated happened between 1985 and April 1989.
- The NSW Supreme Court appointed JB Harkness as liquidator of Spedley Holdings and Spedley Securities on 25 May 1989.
- Greater Pacific Investments went into liquidation on 24 July 1989, by order of the Supreme Court of Victoria.
- GPI Leisure collapsed because of its exposure to the Spedley companies. That exposure included a $100 million investment in Spedley Holdings and a $100 million guarantee given to Spedley Securities.

ASIC's findings on the causes are blunt. The report says the accounts of Spedley Securities were "window dressed" around balance date, and that Yuill "treated the companies' funds as his own." It also says the auditors did not follow up transactions they had identified as problematic.

The investigation began in 1989. It was run first by the NSW Corporate Affairs Commission and later by the Australian Securities Commission (now ASIC). The report describes it as one of the regulator's largest ever investigations, with more than 6,500 pages of examination transcript.

## Jamie Craven's role, charge and sentence

ASIC's report lists James Craven as a director of Greater Pacific and GPI Leisure. The Australian Financial Review described him at the time as a former Spedley Securities director. In a 1999 column, the AFR's Trevor Sykes described him as Spedley's former manager of corporate finance.

His prosecution came out of what ASIC calls the "Bisley Rights Brief." In early 1988, Greater Pacific had to cover a large shortfall in a rights issue by Bisley Investment. ASIC's report says that on Craven's instructions, Spedley Securities paid $21,989,977.60 to BNZ Securities to meet Greater Pacific's sub-underwriting obligations.

According to ASIC:

- Craven was charged on 15 March 1993.
- He was committed to stand trial in January 1994.
- In June 1995, he pleaded guilty to one charge under section 229(1) of the Companies Code, a provision covering company officers' duties.
- He was sentenced to nine months' imprisonment, with a minimum term of six months and 23 days.

The AFR's report of 7 June 1995 says he pleaded guilty in the NSW Supreme Court to improperly using his position to raise $21.9 million through Spedley for another company.

ASIC's report also says he was disqualified from managing a company for five years, from 3 August 1990 to 3 August 1995. It adds that his conviction would bar him from managing a corporation for five years after his release from prison.

Separately, the AFR reported in November 1994 that Craven had been found guilty on three of nine contempt-of-court charges. The charges were over his refusal to answer questions as a witness at Brian Yuill's trial. Later reports put his total time in prison at about a year, counting the contempt matter:

- Crikey (2001) wrote that he was jailed for six months for contempt and then six months for breaching directors' duties.
- The AFR (1999) wrote that he was "jailed for a year on charges of fraud and contempt of court."

I could not open the court's contempt judgment to confirm the exact contempt penalty, so treat that detail as reported rather than verified.

The same 1999 AFR column says Craven went bankrupt, that his marriage ended, and that he gave extensive information to the official investigation.

Craven was not the only person jailed. ASIC lists sentences for Brian Yuill and for director John Corner. The report says that "Yuill, Craven and Corner - all received significant jail terms."

## The "1980s" detail

Ed Craven's Wikipedia page says Jamie Craven was jailed "in the 1980s," and some news profiles tie the jailing loosely to "the late 1980s collapse." The record doesn't support a 1980s jailing. The collapse was in 1989. ASIC's report and contemporary AFR coverage date the contempt finding to November 1994 and the guilty plea and sentence to June 1995.

The "six months" figure most likely reflects the minimum term (six months and 23 days) or the separately reported contempt sentence. The head sentence for the directors'-duties charge was nine months.

## The father-son link, and why it's back in the news

The family connection is a matter of public reporting:

- The Guardian (Nino Bucci, August 2022) reported that Ed Craven's father is James Ashley Craven, "who was jailed and bankrupted only years before his son was born" because of his involvement in the Spedley group collapse. The Guardian also reported that the two have been involved in some of the same companies and that there are no restrictions on Jamie Craven being a company director.
- The Sydney Morning Herald (Kishor Napier-Raman, 22 July 2026) also identified Jamie Craven as Ed Craven's father.

Ed Craven was born in 1995 and co-founded Stake and Kick. The SMH reported that he became Australia's youngest billionaire in 2023, at 27, and estimated his wealth at $4.6 billion.

I couldn't find a single new event in October 2026 that revived the Spedley story. The most recent reputable coverage I found is the SMH's July 2026 report on a fire at a beachside house in Korora, north of Coffs Harbour, owned by Jamie Craven's corporate interests. NSW Police treated the fire as "suspicious." The SMH stated that "there is no suggestion the Cravens are under any suspicion relating to the fire." The Spedley history mostly resurfaces as background whenever Ed Craven is profiled, and the "1980s" wording circulates widely through his Wikipedia entry.

## Response and comment

I found no public comment from Jamie Craven or Ed Craven about the Spedley case in recent coverage:

- The SMH said Ed Craven's representatives were contacted about the July 2026 fire. Its article did not include a response.
- In 2022, The Guardian said Ed Craven could not be reached for comment.

The only quote from Jamie Craven I found is from the 1999 AFR interview, as cited by The Guardian: "I have a boarding school background and I have no doubt that equipped me very well to handle life in prison."
