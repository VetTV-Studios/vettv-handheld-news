Xbox is going after "triple stacks," the practice of releasing the same game three times on the Microsoft Store, once each for Xbox Series X|S, Xbox One and Windows PC, with a separate achievement list for each.

## What changed

TrueAchievements reports that, in an email to developers it has seen, Xbox said releasing identical games that "don't provide distinct experiences" will violate section 10.1 of Microsoft Store policy, "Distinct Function & Value; Accurate Representation." According to the report, Xbox also warned that as it enforces the rule, it may restrict duplicate releases from automatically getting access to the wider Xbox ecosystem.

Microsoft hasn't published a public statement on the change. Everything about the email comes from TrueAchievements' reporting.

## What developers should do instead

The email, as reported, gives developers two options for shipping a game across platforms:

- **Xbox Play Anywhere:** one product that covers supported Xbox consoles and Windows PCs.
- **Game binding:** separate products that share one Xbox services setup, so achievements, saves and multiplayer carry across.

Microsoft's own GDK documentation describes game binding as two or more products sharing the same Xbox services configuration, set up in Partner Center through a Microsoft account manager. Once the secondary product has launched, it can't be unbound.

Games that offer a genuinely distinct experience on each platform can still get Xbox services, TrueAchievements says.

## Why it matters

TrueAchievements' data shows how quickly the practice grew. It counted 37 triple stacks in 2024 and 246 in 2025, or 738 individual releases, mostly from publisher Afil Games. 2026 has seen 233 so far, or 699 releases. The site notes that not every multi-version release is shovelware, since some developers ship separate versions for legitimate reasons.

The reported guidance doesn't say when enforcement begins or whether existing triple-stack games will stay on the store.
