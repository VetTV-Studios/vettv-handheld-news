If you're hoping to unwrap a Steam Frame this holiday season, here's the reality check. Valve has given reservation holders their first real timing estimate, and for many of them it's **2027**. Here's what Valve said, and what to do with it.

## Quick recap: how we got here

According to Valve's update (quoted by Playing Tux and VR.org), **signups for Steam Frame opened on September 14**, Valve did a **one-time randomization of that list on September 17**, and **orders started going out on September 18**, in the order people landed after that shuffle. Mixed News reported that signups were split into a **reservation queue** (people whose spot was within the units available to ship) and a **waitlist** (people beyond it).

## If you have a reservation

Valve says you can now check your expected order window by going to the **Steam Frame product page while logged in to your Steam account**. There you'll see whether you'll get your purchase email **"either by December, or in 2027."**

- **"By December":** your purchase email should arrive by the end of this year. As VR.org points out, that's a date for an *email*, not for a headset on your doorstep. You still have to buy it, and it still has to ship.
- **"2027":** Valve says this group **"will receive further (more detailed) information on their expected order timing by January."**

## The 72-hour rule

This is the part not to miss. Valve's post says: **"For everyone receiving a purchase email: You must complete your purchase within 72 hours or we will move to the next person in line."** OC3D and GamingOnLinux both repeated this, and Mixed News reports the same 72-hour expiry in Valve's Limited Drop Program terms.

My advice: make sure the email address on your Steam account is one you actually check every day, and add Valve to your safe-senders list. Three days goes fast.

## If you're on the waitlist

Valve says it will let waitlisted folks know **if spots open up** as it works through the reservation queues. Anyone can still join the waitlist, and new signups go **at the end, in the order they join.**

Two key points from Valve:

- **Queues are per region.** There are **separate queues for each region**, so some regions may start pulling from their waitlist sooner than others.
- **No leapfrogging.** In Valve's words: *"no one on the waitlist is leapfrogging over anyone currently holding a reservation; we are only dipping into waitlists in a region when we reach the end of its reservation queue."*

So if you're waitlisted, there's no timeline yet. Your best chance is people ahead of you letting their 72 hours run out, or a future production run. Valve hasn't announced a date for either.

## What it costs

VR.org reports the store page lists two kits: **$1,059 for 256GB** and **$1,299 for 1TB**. Mixed News also notes that buyers in Japan, Taiwan, and Hong Kong order through Valve's distributor KOMODO instead of the email queue, with South Korea "at a later date." If you're outside those markets, the email is the only way to buy.

## My take

Valve being upfront about "December or 2027" beats leaving people guessing, even if the news isn't what holiday shoppers wanted. If you've got a reservation, log in today, note which group you're in, and keep an eye on your inbox. If you're waitlisted, it's a waiting game for now. Meanwhile, the SteamOS 0.4 update has made the headset better for the people who already have one.
