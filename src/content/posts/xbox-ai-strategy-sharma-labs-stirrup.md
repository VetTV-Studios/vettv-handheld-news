Microsoft's gaming business has spent 2026 trying to explain where it stands on generative AI. Over eight months, its leadership has made public promises, launched a new experimental team, and now an employee's weekend posts on X have pushed the topic back into the headlines. Here's what's on the record, with dates and sources.

## February: new leadership and the "AI slop" promise

On **February 20, 2026**, Microsoft announced that **Asha Sharma** would become **Executive Vice President and CEO, Microsoft Gaming**, reporting to Satya Nadella. **Phil Spencer** retired after 12 years leading the gaming business, and Xbox president **Sarah Bond** left the company. **Matt Booty** was promoted to Executive Vice President and **Chief Content Officer**, reporting to Sharma.

Sharma's previous role was president of Microsoft's CoreAI product, as PCGamesN and TechCrunch noted. Before that, she was Instacart's COO and a vice president at Meta. Microsoft's blog now carries an editor's note saying that as of **April 23, 2026**, Microsoft Gaming is known as XBOX and Sharma's title is **CEO XBOX**.

In her first memo to staff, published on Microsoft's official blog, Sharma wrote: "As monetization and AI evolve and influence this future, we will not chase short-term efficiency or flood our ecosystem with soulless AI slop. Games are and always will be art, crafted by humans, and created with the most innovative technology provided by us."

## Booty: "There are no directives on AI coming down"

A few days later, Sharma and Booty sat down with **Windows Central's Jez Corden**. **GamesIndustry.biz** reported on the interview on **February 25, 2026**, in a story by Sophie McEvoy. It quotes Booty: "There are no directives on AI coming down. Our teams are free to use any technologies that might be beneficial, whether it's helping write code or check for bugs – things more in the production pipeline. At the end of the day, as Asha said, we're committed to art made by people. Technology is only in support of that."

In the same interview, Sharma said: "I will not flood our ecosystem with slop. We won't have careless output, we won't have derivative work." PCGamesN and Windows Central's own write-up report the same quotes. GamesIndustry.biz headlined its story "Xbox faces 'no pressure from Microsoft' to use AI."

## October 13: Xbox Labs

On **October 13, 2026**, Sharma announced **XBOX LABS** in a note to employees, which Microsoft also published on **Xbox Wire**. She describes it as "a small group of game makers, engineers and scientists whose job is to turn emerging ideas into things our players and developers can experience." Polygon reports that the team is led by Xbox CTO **Scott Van Vliet**.

In the note, Sharma points to what has happened "in recent weeks": games "rapidly decompiled and rebuilt" for new hardware, Windows PC builds translated for Apple Silicon or run in a browser through WebAssembly and WebGPU, and big games "remixed into the source code of another." She says she sees "possibilities in making great games easier to access, giving old games new life and allowing more players to become creators and co-creators." The team will "build in the open," she writes, and measure success "by whether more people are playing and creating."

She also writes that "coding became the first major workload for agents" and that games share many of the same properties. She says Xbox will share the tools it builds with developers, and that its priorities "aren't changing," including "being responsible stewards of IP."

**How outlets read it:**
- **VGC** said Xbox formed the team "in response to the recent explosion in AI game modding and recompilations."
- **The Verge** called it Xbox "embracing the chaos of AI-powered game ports."
- **Polygon** said it "appears to be something of a reversal" of Sharma's February stance.
- **Pure Xbox and Polygon** both noted that the note doesn't use the words "generative AI" or "vibe coding."

## October 18–19: an Xbox strategist says "We're using AI"

Over the weekend, **Brandon Stirrup**, a Senior Business Strategy Manager at Microsoft working on Xbox, posted a series of messages on X. Windows Central, GamesRadar+ and Pure Xbox reported on them. I read the posts on his account directly. All times below are CT.

- **Oct 18, 2:14 PM:** "I vibe coded a bad but improvable game in two days and I only spent 3 hours actively."
- **Oct 18, 6:14 PM:** "Look if I'm fired tomorrow it will be because I told you the truth that nobody wants you to know. You can make a game with a simple prompt and yes you can make custom, tailored games."
- **Oct 18, 6:17 PM:** "I'm happy to take the hit if it means hundreds at Xbox can stop pretending that they're not using AI. It's time to accept and move forward."
- **Oct 18, 7:02–7:54 PM:** In a run of posts, he wrote that game developers "refusing to believe that AI has a place in your future" should "not be in the business of being a business." He also wrote that "Gaming is the only industry slow-walking the transition because users have negative sentiment" and "Users want the truth. We're using AI."
- **Oct 18, 8:24 PM:** "I love Xbox and Microsoft and I have spent 10+ years proving this. My goal is to get this over with so we can move on as a company, despite the fact that some will persecute me for this: we use AI, just as all companies do in 2026."

He also replied to a commenter that having a "human in the loop, exercising the judgment" is "mandatory."

These are one employee's personal posts. They aren't a company announcement. Stirrup doesn't say his posts reflect any internal dispute, and none of the outlets I read report one. GamesRadar+ notes it "can't verify his statement on how many people at Xbox use AI." As of my check on Monday morning, I found no public response from Microsoft. I also found no report that Microsoft has disciplined him.

## Microsoft's earlier gaming AI work

AI at Xbox isn't new. Here's some earlier context:

- **Muse (WHAM), February 19, 2025:** Microsoft Research and Xbox's Ninja Theory published Muse, a "World and Human Action Model" trained on Bleeding Edge gameplay, in the journal Nature. Xbox Wire pitched it as a tool for gameplay ideation, and said it could help make older games accessible on new devices.
- **Gaming Copilot, 2025:** An AI assistant in the Xbox Game Bar. It rolled out in beta to Xbox Insiders on PC on August 6, 2025. Xbox Wire then announced a wider beta on Windows PC and the Xbox mobile app on September 18, 2025.

## The reaction

There has been criticism at every step:

- **Sharma's appointment:** PCGamesN wrote that it "has been met with plenty of concern" because of her ties to Microsoft's AI push. Windows Central's own writer admitted to being "rather terrified" until the Booty and Sharma interview.
- **Xbox Labs:** Polygon framed it as a possible reversal of the "no AI slop" promise. Sharma's note says the commitments still stand.
- **Stirrup's posts:** GamesRadar+ described his demo as "a basic soccer sim that looks every bit like the three hours he says he put into it." Windows Central's Adam Hales wrote that he hopes Stirrup isn't fired. Hales added that "the issue comes down to how AI is being used," with developers still needing to understand what the AI produces.

## Analysis (opinion)

This is my read, not reporting. The positions aren't necessarily contradictory. Booty's February line was about AI as a production tool, like writing code or catching bugs, and not about AI-generated art. Stirrup's claim that people at Xbox use AI fits that description. Where the messaging gets harder is Xbox Labs. Being excited about players remixing and porting games with AI tools is a different idea from "games crafted by humans." It's reasonable for players to want Xbox to explain where the line is. To be fair to Microsoft, Labs is days old and hasn't shipped anything yet. To be fair to the critics, "we'll build in the open" is a promise, and so far there's nothing concrete to judge.
